# Teaching and explanation review

Start with a concrete problem, make one decision visible, demonstrate the result, then name its costs or limits. For example, the demo asks what Delivery requires, highlights a constructor parameter, draws the matching dependency edge, then points out the setup work and the limit of visual inspection.

For real Roemer episodes, start from an actual gameplay scenario and trace the relevant caller or authored content before writing the explanation. Cite source paths and relevant revisions in the brief. Separate observations from design intent and proposals. Do not infer that a technique is implemented merely because illustrative code compiles or a diagram looks plausible.

The brief records audience, central question, learning outcome, demonstration, tradeoffs, and unverified claims. The script maps narration to named scenes and visual cues. Keep jargon proportional to the audience, show a small readable example, and leave a pause after introducing a new relationship. Technical and narrative review are human-led; scaffolded TODOs are not finished teaching material.

Before recording, review every technical claim against source or a reproducible demonstration. Before publishing, inspect stills for legibility and the full clip for pacing; listen to any recorded narration and check it against the visuals. Record unresolved claims explicitly instead of turning them into confident narration. This foundation intentionally does not automate script approval, narration generation, or publishing.
